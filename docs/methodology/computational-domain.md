# Computational Domain

The computational setup, presented in {numref}`fig-config` and
adapted from {cite:t}`schuch2020thesis`, is based on the previous work
of {cite:t}`schuch2018three`, however, a few improvements are
proposed. The test section (TS) is the region where
the flow is analyzed. The bed slope $S$ is included in the
computational domain via an immersed boundary method (IBM), following
the alternating direction forcing strategy proposed by
{cite:t}`Gautier2014`.

```{figure} figures/plunge-config.svg
:name: fig-config
:width: 100%

Schematic representation of the computational domain (not to scale).
Spanwise coordinate $x_3$ is perpendicular to the plane. It is divided
in four sections: in gray, the tilted bed is inserted by the immersed
boundary method (IBM); TS represents the test section, where the flow
is analyzed; SZ$_a$ represents the sponge zone upstream of TS, it
provides a turbulent inflow; SZ$_b$ represents the sponge zone
downstream of TS, employed in order to damp the turbulence level and
avoid reflections from the outflow boundary. The two coefficients
$\sigma_{abu}$ and $\sigma_{abc}$, that are employed at SZ$_b$, are
shown for reference.
```

## Domain dimensions

The streamwise extent of the domain is fixed for all cases:
the recycling sponge zone SZ$_a$ has length $L_{1a} = 25$, the test
section spans $0 \leq x_1 \leq L_1 = 250$ and the outflow sponge zone
SZ$_b$ has length $L_{1b} = 62.5$, discretized with $n_1 = 1081$ mesh
points. The bed becomes flat at $x_1 = 297.5$, upstream of the outflow
boundary (dashed line in {numref}`fig-dimensions`).

The vertical extent, on the other hand, depends on the bed slope $S$:
the domain must be tall enough to contain the initial depth $h_0 = 1$,
the total drop of the ramp, and a bottom layer of thickness $L_{2b}$
underneath it (inserted by the IBM). With a uniform vertical mesh
spacing $\Delta x_2 = 0.140625$, the number of points $n_2$ is the
smallest value supported by the solver's mesh that fits these
constraints:

## Sponge zones

Two sponge zones are employed for different reasons. Firstly, SZ$_a$
is located upstream of the test section in order to provide a
turbulent inflow condition by means of a recycling technique. It
eliminates problems with transition to turbulence and the
re-circulation bubble near the inlet. To this end, the inflow boundary
condition (where $x_1 = -L_{1a}$) for the velocity field is defined as

```{math}
:label: eq-inflow-v
u_i(-L_{1a}, x_2, x_3) = u_i(-L_{1a}^*, x_2, x_3).
```

Furthermore, a forcing term is designed to compensate the viscous
dissipation and maintain a constant flow rate at the recycling zone.
It is added to the right-hand side of the momentum equation
{eq}`eq-ns` and written as

```{math}
:label: eq-sza-forcing
f_{i,sza}(x_i) =
\begin{cases}
u_1 \left( \dfrac{V_{sza}}{\int_{V_{sza}} u_1 \, dV} - 1 \right)
\delta_{i1} & \text{if } x_1 \leq 0, \\
0 & \text{otherwise},
\end{cases}
```

where $V_{sza}$ is the volume comprehended by sponge zone A.

Secondly, SZ$_b$ applies near the outflow boundary an intrinsic
profile for the streamwise velocity $u_1(x_i)$, in order to vanish
turbulence levels. As suggested by {cite:t}`henniger2010direct`, the
numerical domain should be long enough to promote the deposition of
the entire suspended material, however, that is very computationally
demanding. Following {cite:t}`schuch2018three`, an absorption
coefficient is defined in order to remove suspended concentration
upstream of the outlet, significantly decreasing computational costs.

Three hyperbolic tangent functions are defined in order to guarantee a
smooth transition near the outflow boundary. A transition in time
$\sigma_{abt}$ is defined as a function of the time when the front
reaches the outlet $t_{ab}$ and the size of the time window used for
the moving time average $t_{size} = 3{,}000 \Delta t$. As a
consequence, SZ$_b$ only takes place after the front leaves the
computational domain and the flow is in a quasi-steady state. Then,
two spatial coefficients, $\sigma_{abu}$ and $\sigma_{abc}$, are used
for streamwise velocity and particle concentration, respectively.
They are written as

```{math}
:label: eq-sponge-zone
\begin{align}
\sigma_{abt}(t)   &= \dfrac{1}{2}
  \left[ 1 + \tanh(t - t_{ab} - 2 t_{size}) \right], \\
\sigma_{abu}(x_1) &= \dfrac{1}{2}
  \left[ 1 + \tanh(x_1 - L_1 - L_{1b} + 3h) \right], \\
\sigma_{abc}(x_1) &= \dfrac{1}{2}
  \left[ 1 + \tanh(x_1 - L_1 - L_{1b} + 1h) \right],
\end{align}
```

besides that, they are schematically presented in
{numref}`fig-config`. In order to impose SZ$_b$ into the governing
equations, two forcing terms are defined, one for the streamwise
velocity $f_{abu}$ and another for the suspended concentration
$f_{abc}$. They are written as

```{math}
:label: eq-szb-forcing
\begin{align}
f_{i,abu}(x_i,t) &= \sigma_{abt}(t)\, \sigma_{abu}(x_1)
  \left[ u_1^*(x_2) - u_1(x_i) \right] \delta_{1i}, \\
f_{abc}(x_i,t)   &= - \sigma_{abt}(t)\, \sigma_{abc}(x_1)\, c(x_i),
\end{align}
```

and respectively added to the right-hand side of the momentum equation
{eq}`eq-ns` and the scalar transport equation
{eq}`eq-scalar-transport`. The vertical streamwise velocity profile
$u_1^*(x_2)$ is obtained by means of a spanwise average and a moving
average in time at a reference position, upstream of the outflow
boundary.

## Boundary conditions

A nondeformable water surface is assumed for the top boundary
condition (where $x_2 = 0$), which is described by a no-flux condition
for the particle concentration and a free-slip condition for the
velocity field, following {cite:t}`nasr13`,

```{math}
:label: eq-top-bc-scalar
\frac{1}{Re\, Sc} \frac{\partial c(x_1, 0, x_3)}{\partial x_2}
+ e^g_2\, u_s\, c(x_1, 0, x_3) = 0,
```

```{math}
:label: eq-top-bc-velocity
\left[
\dfrac{\partial u_1(x_1, 0, x_3)}{\partial x_2},\;
u_2(x_1, 0, x_3),\;
\dfrac{\partial u_3(x_1, 0, x_3)}{\partial x_2}
\right] = [0, 0, 0].
```

At the solid-fluid interface $x_{2r}$, a convective outflow condition
reproduces the particle deposition {cite:p}`necker2002high` and a
no-slip condition is used for the carrier fluid:

```{math}
:label: eq-bottom
\begin{align}
\dfrac{\partial c(x_1, x_{2r}, x_3)}{\partial t}
+ e^g_2\, u_s
\dfrac{\partial c(x_1, x_{2r}, x_3)}{\partial x_2} &= 0, \\
u_i(x_1, x_{2r}, x_3) &= [0, 0, 0].
\end{align}
```

The inflow condition for the velocity field was defined in Equation
{eq}`eq-inflow-v`. Besides that, a vertical concentration profile is
proposed

```{math}
:label: eq-inflow-profile
I(x_2) = \frac{1}{2}
\left( 1 - \tanh\left( 2\, Re\, Sc\, u_s (x_2 - 1) \right) \right),
```

notice that it is in agreement with the top boundary condition for the
scalar field {eq}`eq-top-bc-scalar`. It is then normalized and applied
to the entire recycling zone (SZ$_a$, where $x_1 < 0$), as follows

```{math}
:label: eq-inflow-concentration
c(x_1 < 0, x_2, x_3) = I(x_2) + 1
- \dfrac{1}{h} \left( \int_0^h I(x_2)\, dx_2 \right).
```

Regular 1-D convection equations are employed at the outflow boundary
($x_1 = L_1 + L_{1b}$) for the scalar and for the velocity

```{math}
:label: eq-outflow
\begin{align}
\dfrac{\partial c(L_1 + L_{1b}, x_2, x_3)}{\partial t}
+ U_c \dfrac{\partial c(L_1 + L_{1b}, x_2, x_3)}{\partial x_1} &= 0, \\
\dfrac{\partial u_i(L_1 + L_{1b}, x_2, x_3)}{\partial t}
+ U_c \dfrac{\partial u_i(L_1 + L_{1b}, x_2, x_3)}{\partial x_j} &= 0.
\end{align}
```

Notice that the convective equations are only relevant during the
transient part of the simulation, while $\sigma_{abt} < 1$, from
{eq}`eq-sponge-zone`. A periodic boundary condition is assumed for
concentration and velocity in the spanwise direction

```{math}
:label: eq-periodic
\begin{align}
c(x_1, x_2, 0)   &= c(x_1, x_2, L_3), \\
u_i(x_1, x_2, 0) &= u_i(x_1, x_2, L_3).
\end{align}
```

## Initial condition

For the initial condition (when $t = 0$) the domain is defined as
fresh water ($c = 0$) at rest ($u_i = 0$). However, in order to
implement the recycling technique, the velocity field is initialized
at SZ$_a$ (where $x_1 \leq 0$ and $x_2 \leq h$) with white noise (1%
of amplitude), in addition to a vertical profile for the streamwise
velocity

```{math}
:label: eq-initial-profile
u_1(x_1 \leq 0, x_2 \leq h, x_3) =
\tanh \left( \frac{\sqrt{\pi}}{0.01} \left[ x_2 - h \right] \right).
```
