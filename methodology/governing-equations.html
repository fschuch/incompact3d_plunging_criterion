Governing Equations#

Note

The methodology chapters are adapted from the PhD thesis of Schuch [2020], which describes the numerical framework employed in the article [Schuch et al., 2021].

The present framework is based on the resolution of the incompressible Navier-Stokes equations. \(N\) scalar transport equations under the Boussinesq approximation are used for sediments with different grain sizes. The incompressible Navier-Stokes equations are written in their dimensionless form as

(1)#\[\frac{\partial u_j}{\partial x_j} = 0,\]
(2)#\[\frac{\partial u_i}{\partial t} + u_j \frac{\partial u_i}{\partial x_j} = - \frac{\partial p}{\partial x_i} + \frac{1}{Re} \frac{\partial^2 u_i}{\partial x_j \partial x_j} + e^g_i \frac{1}{{Fr_0}^2} \sum_{\ell=1}^N c_\ell,\]
(3)#\[\frac{\partial c_\ell}{\partial t} + \left( u_j + u_{s,\ell}\, e^g_j \right) \frac{\partial c_\ell}{\partial x_j} = \frac{1}{Re\, Sc} \frac{\partial^2 c_\ell}{\partial x_j \partial x_j}, \quad \ell = 1, \dots, N,\]

where \(u_i\), \(p\) and \(c\) correspond to velocity, pressure and portion of each particle size, respectively, together with the coordinate system \(x_i\) (see the computational domain), time \(t\), and the unit vector pointing in the gravity direction \(e^g = [0, 1, 0]\). The settling velocity for each particle size is \(u_s\) and may be considered constant. It is related to the particle diameter by the Stokes settling velocity law [Julien, 1998], which assumes that the dominant flow force on an individual particle is the Stokes drag.

The Reynolds, densimetric Froude and Schmidt numbers at the inlet and the Stokes settling velocity are the four dimensionless parameters, defined as follows:

(4)#\[\begin{split}\begin{align} Re &= \dfrac{\tilde{Q}_0}{\tilde{\nu}}, \\ Fr &= \dfrac{\tilde{Q}_0}{\sqrt{R \tilde{C}_0 \tilde{g} \tilde{h}_0^3}}, \\ Sc &= \dfrac{\tilde{\nu}}{\tilde{D}}, \\ u_{s,\ell} &= \dfrac{\tilde{u}_{s,\ell} \tilde{h}_0}{\tilde{Q}_0} = \dfrac{\tilde{d}_{s,\ell}^2 R \tilde{g} \tilde{h}_0} {18 \tilde{\nu} \tilde{Q}_0}, \quad \ell = 1, \dots, N, \end{align}\end{split}\]

where \(\tilde{\cdot}\) corresponds to dimensional quantities. \(\tilde{Q}_0\) is the volumetric discharge per unit width, defined as the product of the inlet velocity \(\tilde{U}_0\) with the initial depth \(\tilde{h}_0\). The kinematic viscosity is \(\tilde{\nu}\); the fresh water and sediment densities are \(\tilde{\rho}_w\) and \(\tilde{\rho}_s\), with \(R\) defined as \((\tilde{\rho}_s - \tilde{\rho}_w)/\tilde{\rho}_w\). The gravity acceleration is \(\tilde{g}\), the constant diffusivity of particle concentration is \(\tilde{D}\) and the grain size is \(\tilde{d}_\ell\).

All parameters and variables are made dimensionless using the influx velocity \(\tilde{U}_0\), depth \(\tilde{h}_0\) and sediment concentration \(\tilde{C}_0\) (the associated dimensionless parameters are \(h_0\), \(U_0\) and \(C_0\)).
